解释（待核实）

Coverage: dataset spans 2010-12-01 to 2011-12-09, so both June 2011 and July 2011 are fully covered; no partial-period caveat applies. Policy retail-gbp-v1, GBP; gross_sales = eligible positive-quantity sales excluding cancellation invoices; time is source local wall time with no timezone inferred. Basis: complete calendar months, disjoint and exclusive end dates. Headline (ev_84fbe...): gross_sales fell from the June baseline to the July current period, a negative delta of -42,518.7090 GBP. Country contribution (ev_d0ed98..., top5 + OTHER, 24 groups in OTHER; reconciled totals match the headline delta exactly): - Netherlands: large negative contribution, with current-period value collapsing to near zero. - Australia: large negative contribution. - EIRE: positive contribution, the only top-5 country to grow. - United Kingdom: negative contribution; it is by far the largest base, so its modest relative move still yields a sizeable absolute drag. - France: negative contribution. - OTHER (24 countries combined): positive contribution, partially offsetting the declines. Descriptive summary: the decline is concentrated in a few countries (Netherlands, Australia, UK, France), partly offset by EIRE and the OTHER group. These are additive descriptive contributions only; the breakdown does not establish why any country moved.

待验证假设

  • The Netherlands near-total drop in July may reflect a one-off large order or bulk purchase in June rather than a sustained demand change; would require invoice-level inspection to test.
  • Australia's decline could reflect seasonal or order-timing effects (large infrequent orders) rather than lost customers.
  • EIRE's growth may reflect a shift in order timing or a new/expanded account; not verifiable from aggregate country totals.
  • The UK decline, being the dominant base, may be driven by broad small changes across many customers rather than a single event.
  • Bookkeeping and return adjustments remain in source data per policy caveat; some movement may be accounting artifacts rather than real demand shifts.
