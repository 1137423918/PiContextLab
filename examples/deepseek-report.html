解释（待核实）

Coverage: the dataset spans 2010-12-01 to 2011-12-09, so both October 2011 [2011-10-01,2011-11-01) and November 2011 [2011-11-01,2011-12-01) are fully covered; no partial-period truncation affects either month. Policy retail-gbp-v1: net_sales is signed eligible quantity × unit_price (unit_price > 0, cancellation invoices with nonnegative quantity excluded, duplicate source lines preserved); it is not cash collected, and bookkeeping/return adjustments remain in source. Metric comparison (calendar_month basis): net_sales rose from October to November 2011, with a positive delta (see evidence ev_a053...). The direction of change is an increase. Country breakdown (top 5 plus remainder, additive and reconciled to the total delta): the United Kingdom is the dominant positive contributor, its delta alone exceeding the overall net change, meaning other countries collectively offset part of the UK gain. Among the top-5, Netherlands and Australia are the largest negative contributors, while EIRE and Japan are smaller positive contributors. The remainder (24 other countries combined) is a modest net negative. The reconciled totals match the headline comparison exactly. Interpretation is descriptive only: the figures show where the net_sales change is concentrated by country, not why. The UK's outsized contribution and the offsetting declines in Netherlands and Australia are compositional facts about the change, not established causes.

待验证假设

  • The UK's positive contribution may reflect a genuine demand increase in November, but could also reflect timing of large orders, seasonal pre-holiday stocking, or bookkeeping/return adjustments retained in source.
  • The Netherlands and Australia declines may reflect order timing shifts (large orders landing in October rather than November) rather than lost demand.
  • Because net_sales nets returns and cancellation adjustments, part of the country-level deltas could stem from differing return/cancellation activity between the two months rather than gross sales changes.
  • The remainder group's small net negative could mask offsetting movements among the 24 other countries and should not be read as uniform decline.
